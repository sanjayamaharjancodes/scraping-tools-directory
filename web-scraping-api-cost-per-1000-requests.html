How much does a web scraping API cost per 1,000 requests?

Four APIs, three volumes, from published plans.

Short answer

A web scraping API costs $0.30 to $0.49 per 1,000 basic requests if you make 100,000 requests a month, on the published plans of ScraperAPI, ScrapingBee, Scrapfly and ScrapingDog. At 1,000,000 a month the range drops to $0.09 to $0.149, and at 5,000,000 to $0.07 to $0.12.

Basic means rendering off. ScrapingBee and ScrapingDog render JavaScript by default at 5 credits a page, so you need render_js=false or dynamic=false to get these rates. With rendering, 100,000 pages a month cost $0.90 to $1.49 per 1,000.

What do 1,000 requests cost at different volumes?

The price per 1,000 falls by two thirds to four fifths between 100,000 and 1,000,000 requests a month, because the bigger plans include far more credits for each extra dollar. Each cell shows the cost per 1,000 basic requests and the smallest plan that covers that volume.

These rates assume 1 credit per request, which needs rendering off. ScrapingBee renders by default (render_js is True, 5 credits) until you send render_js=false. ScrapingDog renders by default (5 credits) until you send dynamic=false. ScraperAPI and Scrapfly charge 1 credit unless you turn rendering on. Prices are monthly list prices before annual discounts.

The figure is what you pay per request you make, not per credit you buy. ScrapingBee's Hobby plan ($19) includes only 75,000 credits, so 100,000 requests need the $49 Freelance plan with 250,000 credits. Likewise, Scrapfly's $250 Startup plan stops at 2,500,000 credits, so 5,000,000 requests land on its $500 Enterprise plan.

How much more do JavaScript-rendered requests cost?

Rendered requests use 5 to 10 times as many credits as basic ones. In dollars the jump at 100,000 a month is smaller, about 2 to 3.3 times, because the basic plans leave credits unused. At 100,000 rendered pages a month:

Rendering only where a site needs it is the biggest lever on cost. If only part of your traffic needs rendering, price the two parts separately in the calculator above, or use the ScraperAPI credits calculator for a mixed workload on that one vendor.

What do these per-1,000 figures leave out?

They cover plain pages on default proxies only. Several things raise or lower the real number:

  • Harder targets cost more credits. ScraperAPI charges 5 credits for Amazon, Walmart and eBay and 25 for Google and Bing. Premium proxies cost 10 credits at ScraperAPI and ScrapingDog, 10 or 25 at ScrapingBee, and a Scrapfly residential request costs 25.
  • Failed requests. ScraperAPI's docs say it only charges for successful requests (status 200 and 404) and requests stopped before completion. ScrapingDog says failed requests are never charged. Scrapfly does not bill failed scrapes, but its docs say that protection is disabled and failed scrapes are billed if too many are blocked without its Unblocker. ScrapingBee's pricing page describes rendered and premium charges as applying to successful requests but does not state a rule for basic ones.
  • Overage and custom plans. Scrapfly's Pro, Startup and Enterprise plans continue pay-as-you-go at $3.50, $2.00 and $1.20 per 10k extra credits; the calculator includes this. Other vendors' overage, enterprise quotes and annual discounts are not modeled.
  • Free credits. ScrapingBee offers 1,000 free API credits and Scrapfly's Free plan includes 1,000 credits. They are too small to change a per-1,000 price at these volumes; see web scraping APIs with a free tier.

Price is not the only thing to compare. These figures say nothing about success rates or speed, and this site has not benchmarked them yet (see the methodology).
