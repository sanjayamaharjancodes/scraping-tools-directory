What day are most jobs posted? Four weeks of new jobs on 12,228 company job boards

First-hand counts from the job index behind ATS Jobs Search, made by Scaleturn, which publishes this site. The index reads employers' own career pages on six applicant tracking systems (ATS), and each job carries the posting time from the vendor's feed.

Short answer

Most jobs are posted on a Tuesday, but only just. Of the 117,153 jobs that 12,228 companies posted on their career pages from 19 Oct to 15 Nov 2026, 20.7% went up on a Tuesday once jobs that had already closed are allowed for. Each other weekday had 18.6% to 19.9%, and Saturday and Sunday together 2.9%. Jobs go up during working hours: 68.2% of US jobs with a posting time went up between 9:00 and 17:00 US Eastern time.

Why the raw count is not the answer

A snapshot only holds jobs that are still open, and a job posted on a Monday has had four more days to be filled or taken down than one posted that Friday. That makes later weekdays look busier. In the raw count, Thursday leads with 20.3%, a hair ahead of Tuesday and Friday at 20.1%.

The data shows how large the effect is. The number of jobs from the same weekday falls 13.9% for each week of age, about 2.12% a day. After weighting each day's jobs by that rate, Tuesday leads with 20.7%. Tuesday also leads when each board counts once (21.1%), for US jobs in Eastern time (20.8%) and for European jobs in CET (20.9%). Across all 456,597 open jobs, whatever their age, Tuesday and Wednesday tie at 20.8%. Monday to Wednesday together take 58.1% of the adjusted count.

So the familiar advice that Tuesday is the top posting day holds, but the margin is under one percentage point. For a job seeker, the useful pattern is broader: new jobs arrive on every weekday at a similar rate and almost never at the weekend.

What time are jobs posted?

US jobs go up through the US working day. Few appear before 8:00 Eastern time (0.6% in the hour from 7:00), then 6.4% arrive between 8:00 and 9:00. From 10:00 to 17:00 every hour holds 7.8% to 9.4%, and the busiest three hours run from 12:00 to 15:00 (27.9%). The often-quoted "post at 11 AM" is not a peak here. European jobs follow office hours in CET: 71.6% between 9:00 and 17:00, with the most at 15:00 (10.3%). Workable gives only a date, so it is left out of the hours.

For job-feed builders: when to refresh

If a feed or job board re-reads career pages once a day, the refresh time decides how long a new job waits to be picked up. Across the five ATSs with posting times, postings peak at 15:00 UTC (8.5% in that hour). A daily refresh at 23:00 UTC gives the shortest mean wait, 8.6 hours, and picks up 38.0% of jobs within 6 hours. At 13:00 UTC, just before the US rush, the mean wait is 15.3 hours. A refresh at 19:00 UTC picks up the most within 6 hours, 45.9%. Our own index is built at about 04:00 UTC: a mean wait of 11.4 hours.

Method and limits

  • Source: the ATS Jobs Search index built at 04:03 UTC on 19 Nov 2026 from employers' public job feeds on Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Recruitee, Personio and Workable. It covers the boards that crawl found and could read, not every employer. The board and job totals are its size, not ATS market share.
  • Posting time: the vendor's own field: Greenhouse first_published, Lever createdAt, Ashby publishedAt, Recruitee published_at, Personio createdAt, Workable published_on (a date only). 84 jobs had no date.
  • Copies: a job with the same title, location and posting time on two boards of one ATS counts once. That removed 5,304 jobs, 4,757 of them on Recruitee.
  • Closure adjustment: the daily rate is fitted from how each weekday's count falls from week to week, then each day's jobs are weighted by it. If posting volume itself changed over the four weeks, the rate absorbs that too.
  • Region: read from the location text. 46.5% of open jobs name a US place, 23.1% a European one; the other 30.4% name another region, several regions or no place we recognize. US Eastern and CET include their daylight-saving changes on 1 Nov and 25 Oct.
  • Reproducible: build_data.py rebuilds the CSV and JSON from the index files, refuses any other index build by its SHA-256 hashes, and fails if a number on this page is not in that data. The index files are not published; the CSV and JSON carry every count used. This page was drafted with AI tools from that data.

Search these jobs yourself

ATS Jobs Search searches the same index by title, location and posting date; with its "only jobs not returned before" option, a scheduled run becomes a daily feed of new jobs. Developers can read the feeds directly with the open-source ats-job-feed library. Scaleturn makes both; these are not affiliate links. How reliable those public feeds are at scale is on our ATS job posting API crawl data page.
