Should you apply to a job posted 30 days ago? How old 456,597 open jobs are

First-hand counts from the job index behind ATS Jobs Search, made by Scaleturn, which publishes this site. The index reads employers' own career pages on six applicant tracking systems (ATS). This page counts how long ago each job open on 19 Nov 2026 was posted.

Short answer

Yes, if the employer's own careers page still lists the job and you fit it: a posting 30 days old is younger than most jobs that are open. Of 456,597 jobs open on 18,767 company job boards on 19 Nov 2026, 69.5% had been posted 30 or more days earlier, 43.8% 90 or more days earlier and 15.7% a year or more earlier. Half had been up for 69.5 days or more. Old postings are mostly ordinary vacancies: talent pools and general applications are only 4.9% of the ones a year old. Jobs do come down, though: for every 100 open jobs posted in the last 7 days, 60.3 posted four weeks earlier were still open.

Old postings are mostly real job titles

Talent pools, general applications and "future opportunities" postings collect applications for no single vacancy. There are 5,552 of them (1.2% of open listings) on 3,667 boards, and they are old: half were posted 547.7 days or more before. Yet they are only 4.9% of the 71,572 listings a year old or more. The rest carry ordinary job titles. Those year-old listings are concentrated: half of them sit on 163 boards, 1.9% of the 8,559 boards with one. The three largest, Svetness Personal Training (4,540), BoxLunch & Hot Topic (4,334) and TSMG (2,227), hold 15.5%.

New-looking dates can mislead too. Six boards with 50 or more listings had none posted 14 or more days earlier (7,858 listings): they re-date every listing, so "posted 3 days ago" there says nothing about when the vacancy opened.

If employers posted the same number of jobs every week, these ratios would say that 60.3 in 100 postings are still up four weeks after they go up, fewer than half (48.0) after six weeks, and 25.0 after 13 weeks. Weeks with more or fewer new jobs move the ratios too, so read them as an estimate. They also explain the snapshot: postings that stay up for months pile up, which is why so many open jobs are old.

Groups come from keyword rules on the title; the first rule that matches wins, and 27.5% of listings match none (Other roles). Data and AI listings are older by listing than by board (the median board's median is 62.5 days), so a few large boards hold many old ones.

Titles with a senior word (Senior, Lead, Director and the like) are younger: median 56.3 days, and 7.3% a year or more, against 77.2 days and 18.2% for titles with no seniority word. Junior, intern and student titles fall between, at 69.7 days. US-located listings are younger than Europe-located ones: 58.2 days against 76.2.

Method and limits

  • Source: the ATS Jobs Search index built at 04:03 UTC on 19 Nov 2026 from employers' public job feeds on Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Recruitee, Personio and Workable: 461,985 open jobs on 23,287 boards. 84 jobs with a zero posting date are left out, and a job with the same title, location and posting time on two boards of one ATS counts once (5,304 copies), which leaves 456,597 listings on 18,767 boards. Employers on other systems, such as Workday or iCIMS, are not covered.
  • Open jobs only: jobs that opened and closed before 19 Nov 2026 are not in the index, so these are the ages of open postings, not how long postings last. Long-lived postings weigh more here than among all postings ever made.
  • Rules: role groups, talent pools, seniority words and regions come from keyword rules on the title and location text, so some listings land in the wrong group. Every rule is in the JSON.
  • Reproducible: build_data.py rebuilds the CSV and JSON from the index files, refuses any other index build by its SHA-256 hashes, and fails if a number on this page is not in that data. The index files are not published. This page was drafted with AI tools from that data.

Search these jobs yourself

ATS Jobs Search searches the same index by title keyword, location and posting date, so a search can keep only jobs posted in the last week or month, with links to each employer's page. Developers can read the feeds directly with the open-source ats-job-feed library. Scaleturn makes both; these are not affiliate links. The same index shows what day most jobs are posted and which AI job titles companies post, and our ATS job posting API crawl data shows how reliable the public feeds are.
