How to calculate ScraperAPI credits

The basic formula is successful monthly requests × credits per request = monthly API credits. A normal page request costs 1 credit, but the multiplier rises for harder domains and resource-intensive parameters.

ScraperAPI credit multipliers

ScraperAPI documents 1 credit for a normal request, 5 for e-commerce domains (Amazon, Walmart, eBay), 25 for Google and Bing, including their subdomains, and 30 for LinkedIn. Parameters can also change the cost: rendering, premium routing, and screenshots cost 10 credits; premium plus rendering costs 25; ultra-premium costs 30, or 75 with rendering. Both ultra-premium options are for paid plans only. Accounts on custom discounted pricing may pay more for Ultra Premium, per the docs, so those two presets may not apply to them.

The docs list the render, premium, and screenshot costs under "Extra Cost" without saying whether they include the 1-credit base; pricing.md lists them as 10, which this calculator uses. Confirm with sa-credit-cost or the URL Cost API.

Bot-protected domains need a closer look. The docs and pricing.md both list them at 10 credits, while the pricing page FAQ says such sites "add 10 credits per request." The calculator uses 10, so measure these URLs rather than trusting the preset.

What the estimate can and cannot tell you

Use measured credit cost for production budgeting. ScraperAPI warns that domains may switch protection measures, so a cost can change. Its URL Cost API (requires an API key, so you need an account or the trial) accepts real request parameters, the dashboard's Domain Cost Estimator (called Domain Multiplier in the credit-cost docs) shows the cost for a URL before you scrape, and each response's sa-credit-cost header shows what that request cost. Put the measured result into "Custom" above when accuracy matters.

To stop a request's cost from drifting past what you budgeted, ScraperAPI's pricing.md says you can set max_cost per scrape to keep any single request within your limit.

The estimator multiplies successful requests by credits per request and selects the first published plan whose included credits cover that total. It shows that plan's thread limit but does not size plans by concurrency or geotargeting, and it does not model failed-request rates, retries, yearly discounts, negotiated pricing, pay-as-you-go use, or combinations not explicitly priced in the documentation.

It also ignores limited-offer bonus credits. The pricing page currently shows +250K on Professional and +500K on Advanced, and offers like these can end, so plan thresholds here use the base allowance only.

Frequently asked questions

Do failed requests consume ScraperAPI credits?

No, but treat canceled requests as billable. ScraperAPI charges only 200 and 404 responses plus some canceled requests, and its wording conflicts: the credit-cost docs say requests canceled before 70 seconds, while pricing.md says after 70 seconds.

What happens when credits run out?

By default, scraping pauses until your credits renew. Depending on your plan, you have other options:

  • Auto-upgrade: On Hobby, Startup, Business, Scaling, and Professional, you can opt in to move to the next plan when your credits run out. The next plan's list price is higher, for example Startup is $149/month against Hobby's $49. ScraperAPI's pricing pages don't say how the auto-upgrade charge is calculated or whether it is prorated, so confirm with ScraperAPI before enabling it.
  • Pay-as-you-go: On Scaling, Professional, Advanced, and Enterprise, you can keep scraping at a fixed per-credit rate, up to a monthly cap you set. ScraperAPI's pricing page does not list the rate, so the calculator leaves it out.
  • Annual early renewal: ScraperAPI says annual plans can renew early for a fresh batch of credits.

Is there a free option?

Yes, in two steps. ScraperAPI's pricing.md describes a 7-day trial with 5,000 API credits and no credit card required. When the trial ends, you can upgrade or stay on the Free plan, which includes 1,000 credits per month and 5 concurrent threads. See web scraping APIs with a free tier for how vendors differ.

What if my workload needs more threads than the plan allows?

Credits are not the only limit. Going over your thread limit returns a 429 response, with no extra charge, asking you to bring concurrency back within the plan's limit, so a high-speed job may need a larger plan than its credits suggest.
