ScraperAPI vs ScrapingBee

Both are single-endpoint, credit-based web scraping APIs aimed at developers who don't want to run their own proxy and headless-browser infrastructure. They're close enough in positioning and price that the choice usually comes down to concurrency needs, geotargeting, and which vendor's credit costs fit your traffic mix.

Where they differ in practice

Entry-tier concurrency. At the $49/mo tier, ScrapingBee ships more concurrent requests (50) than ScraperAPI (20) and more credits (250,000 vs. 100,000) for the same headline price — useful if your bottleneck is parallel throughput rather than total monthly volume.

Geotargeting gating. ScraperAPI explicitly restricts geotargeting to the US and EU on its two entry tiers (Hobby $49/mo, Startup $149/mo); global geotargeting requires the $299/mo Business tier or above. ScrapingBee's pricing page lists geotargeting as a feature without publishing which tiers unlock which regions — if geotargeting outside the US/EU matters at a low budget, confirm directly with ScrapingBee before committing.

Both bill by weighted credit, not flat request count. On both vendors, a plain HTTP fetch costs the base credit rate; enabling JavaScript rendering, premium/rotating proxies, or extraction features costs more credits per request. Neither vendor publishes a single "cost per page" number that holds across all use cases — your real monthly cost depends on how often you need the expensive features, not the plan's headline credit count.
