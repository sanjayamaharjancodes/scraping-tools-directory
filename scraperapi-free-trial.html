ScraperAPI free trial: what 5,000 credits buy

Short answer

The ScraperAPI free trial gives you 5,000 API credits for 7 days with no credit card, enough for 5,000 normal requests, 500 rendered pages, 200 Google searches, or 166 LinkedIn requests. ScraperAPI's FAQ counts the 7 days from sign-up, so have your URL list ready first. When the trial ends, you can upgrade or keep scraping on the Free plan's 1,000 credits a month. That is enough to check whether the API returns the data you need, but not to test full volume or sustained concurrency.

How many requests do 5,000 credits buy?

At published single-type costs, between 166 and 5,000. Unpriced combinations can buy fewer. ScraperAPI's credit cost runs from 1 for a normal page to 30 for LinkedIn. The table divides the allowances by each published cost and rounds down to whole requests.

The bot-protected row is flagged because ScraperAPI's sources word it differently. Its credit list says 10 credits, while its pricing FAQ says such sites "add 10 credits per request." Measure those URLs instead of trusting the row.

The docs list ultra_premium as "only available with our Paid Plans," so do not plan on testing it during the trial. Combinations the docs do not price, such as Amazon with rendering, need a measured cost in the calculator's credits-per-request field.

Real tests usually mix types, so budget them by adding up. For example, 200 Amazon pages, 40 Google searches, 100 rendered pages, and 1,000 normal pages each use 1,000 credits. That totals 4,000 credits and leaves 1,000 for reruns.

Is the trial enough for a real test?

At these costs, 166 to 5,000 requests can be enough for a data-quality sample, but they are not a volume test. The whole allowance is 5% of one month on the cheapest paid plan, Hobby, which costs $49 for 100,000 credits.

Sustained concurrency is also hard to judge. The Free plan allows 5 concurrent threads and Hobby allows 20, but the pricing pages and docs checked for this page state no thread limit for the trial. A 429 means you sent too many simultaneous requests.

The same pages do not state the trial's geotargeting. Hobby lists US & EU only, so a test from another country may not predict what that plan delivers.

A sensible way to spend the credits:

  • Look up costs first. The docs offer the Domain Multiplier in your dashboard and the /account/urlcost endpoint to check a URL's credit cost before you scrape.
  • Confirm with real requests. Run a few per domain and enter the sa-credit-cost response header's value in the calculator.
  • Cap the cost. Set max_cost so that a request above your limit is rejected with a 403. Exhausted credits also return a 403, but only the cap's message says "This request exceeds your max_cost." Check your balance after one rejected request to see whether it was charged.
  • Check the output. Compare returned pages with what you need before you scale the sample.

What happens when the credits run out?

ScraperAPI's pricing page says "scraping pauses until your credits renew." Its status-code docs list a 403, "You have exhausted the API Credits available in this monthly cycle." Neither mentions the trial specifically.

When the 7 days end, ScraperAPI says you can upgrade to a paid plan or keep scraping for free. The Free plan allows 1,000 credits a month.

The pages checked do not address unused trial credits, so plan as if they do not carry over.

If you need more room to finish a test, you have two published routes:

  • Ask for extra credits. The docs say to contact support if you need additional credits for testing. They publish no amount.
  • Buy one month of Hobby. That is the paid route, at $49 for 100,000 credits.

Separately, the pricing page states a general 7-day no-questions-asked refund policy.

Do failed requests use trial credits?

No, ScraperAPI says it charges only for successful requests, which it defines as 200 and 404 status codes. Two details still affect your budget:

  • A 404 costs credits. Clean your URL list before you test, or dead links will use up the allowance.
  • Canceling early can cost credits. The docs also charge for requests you cancel before ScraperAPI has had 70 seconds to finish them, so keep your client timeout at 70 seconds or longer.

What these numbers do not cover

The counts assume one request type, published credit costs, and no reruns. ScraperAPI's costs can differ for your exact target URL, which is why the header reading matters more than the table. Nothing here measures success rates or speed, because this page reports published terms and no benchmark has been run.
